    How we score

    The ethics score, explained

    Heat doesn't fall on everyone equally. The people waiting at bus stops, walking to school or working outside take the most of it. The ethics score asks whether a design protects those people, not only whether it lowers a temperature.

    Six criteria

    Weights add up to 100%

    Working out the score

    Each criterion gets a score out of 100. We multiply each by its weight and add them up.

    score = 0.25 × safety + 0.15 × access + 0.20 × environment + 0.15 × cost + 0.15 × equity + 0.10 × vulnerable
    ScoreGradeMeaning
    90 to 100AProtects people well. Keep it maintained.
    75 to 89BGood, with a few gaps to close.
    60 to 74CWorks for some people and leaves others exposed.
    45 to 59DNeeds changes before it's acceptable.
    Below 45FPuts people at risk. Act now.

    Standards we draw on

    • ASCE Code of Ethics. Engineers put the safety, health and welfare of the public first.
    • IEEE 7000-2021. A process for spotting ethical concerns early in system design and building them into the requirements.
    • ASTM E1980. How to calculate the solar reflectance index (SRI) of a surface.
    • ISO 14040/44. How to compare the environmental impact of materials over their whole life.

    What this tool can't do

    HeatShield is a student capstone project. It is not a stamped engineering assessment, and nothing here should go into a permit or a contract without review by a licensed engineer.

    • It doesn't measure temperature. The sun absorption view is based on how bright each part of your photo is, which is only a rough guide to how much sun a surface absorbs.
    • The photo check can tell an outdoor place from a selfie or an indoor shot, but it can't tell a road from a roof. You pick the type, and we show our worked example for it.
    • We don't analyze photos of people. If someone fills most of the frame, the photo is turned away.
    • The audit records and examples are demonstration data for Los Angeles sites.
